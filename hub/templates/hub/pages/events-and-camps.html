    The thing nobody explains

    A camp during a dead period cannot get him seen

    Families spend real money on camps and showcases sold on "exposure." Whether that exposure is even legally possible depends on the date, because the NCAA divides the year into periods that control what college coaches may do.

    This is the most useful thing on this page: the calendar is published, it's free, and checking it takes two minutes. An event held when coaches cannot attend may still be excellent training — but it is not a recruiting opportunity, and it should not be priced like one.

    The question to ask any camp organiser: "What recruiting period does this fall in, and are college coaches permitted to attend and evaluate?" An organiser who can't answer that precisely is selling something other than exposure.

    The framework

    Four periods, defined by the NCAA

    These are the NCAA's own definitions.1,2

    Dead period no contact, no watching

    A time when it is not permissible to make in-person recruiting contacts or evaluations on or off campus, and official or unofficial visits to campus are not permitted.1 An event in a dead period offers no coach evaluation of any kind.

    Quiet period on campus only

    In-person contact is permissible only on the college's own campus. No off-campus contacts or evaluations may be made.1 Useful for campus visits; useless for a showcase in a rented gym.

    Evaluation period coaches may assess

    A time when authorised athletics staff may be involved in off-campus activities to assess academic qualifications and playing ability.1 This is when coaches can be in the stands. Evaluation, however, is not contact.

    Contact period coaches may talk

    The period when in-person off-campus contact with the athlete and family is permitted, in addition to evaluation. The 2026–27 Division I calendar sets these out by date.2

    Read the calendar, don't take dates from here

    The 2026–27 Division I men's basketball cycle opened with a dead period from 1 to 19 August 2026 and a quiet period from 20 August to 8 September 2026.3 Those are the only dates this page states, and only to show the shape of it.

    The rest of the year — every evaluation window, every dead period around signing dates and championships — is in the official NCAA PDF, which is free and authoritative.2 Download it, put the evaluation windows in a family calendar, and plan spending around them. Secondary summaries of this calendar are everywhere and they go stale.

    The gate

    Without a BBCS account he cannot play in certified events

    This is the single most actionable item on the page, and it catches families out every spring.

    Every athlete participating in an NCAA-certified non-scholastic event must have an active account in the NCAA Basketball Certification System (BBCS).4,5 Non-scholastic means outside the high school season — club, AAU, circuit and most summer events.

    • All tabs on the athlete account must be complete, including document uploads where applicable, before a coach can invite the athlete to the team's bench or roster.4
    • Parents and guardians create the account — not coaches.4 If a club coach offers to handle it, that's a flag.
    • BBCS works on a "bench" concept: a pool of eligible athletes and assistant coaches, from which event rosters are drawn.4
    • Players and coaches not on the BBCS team roster are not allowed to participate.6 No exceptions on the day.

    Certification also runs at the event level: for an event to be certified, teams and participants must register, hold the relevant memberships, and meet NCAA requirements — and those must be completed by all teams attending.6

    Guidance — not a rule

    Do the BBCS registration now, in the off-season, not the week before an April event. Document uploads take time, and an incomplete account means he sits in the parking lot while his team plays in front of coaches. It costs nothing to be ready early.

    Sorting the options

    Three kinds of event, three different purposes

    1. His high school season

      Scholastic competition, and the baseline. Coaches evaluate during permitted periods. It costs nothing extra and it's the most honest record of who he is as a player, over months rather than a weekend.

    2. NCAA-certified non-scholastic events

      Club and circuit basketball, concentrated in the spring and summer evaluation windows. This is where most college evaluation of prospects actually happens. Requires BBCS registration and a certified event. The cost varies enormously by program.

    3. College-run camps

      Held on a specific college's campus and run by its staff. The value is narrow but real: that staff sees him directly, and a quiet period doesn't block on-campus activity. It is a way to be seen by one program — worth it if he genuinely wants that program, poor value as a scattergun.

    The exposure-for-money warning

    A commercial showcase that is not NCAA-certified, is not held in an evaluation period, and is not run by a college, is selling a weekend of basketball. That can be worth paying for on its own terms — reps, competition, video. It is not recruiting exposure, whatever the marketing says.

    Before paying, ask for three things in writing: the event's NCAA certification status, the recruiting period the dates fall in, and which college programs attended last year. Vague answers to any of the three are your answer.

    Club basketball

    The circuits, and how a player actually gets on one

    Non-scholastic basketball is organised into shoe circuits, and which circuit a club team plays on largely determines how many college coaches see it. The five principal ones are Nike EYBL, adidas 3SSB, Under Armour Association (UAA), PUMA Pro16 and New Balance P32.7

    • Nike EYBL is generally considered the most prestigious, with Peach Jam as its signature event. For a player being recruited by Division I programs, it's the circuit coaches watch most closely. Nike also runs a lower entry tier, the EYBL Champions League, from which teams can earn a place in the main league.7
    • adidas 3SSB features top national talent with strong college coach attendance across the spring.7
    • Under Armour Association has grown substantially and is known for producing mid-major and high-major offers.7

    You cannot apply, and you cannot buy in

    This is the correction that matters most, because the opposite is widely assumed. Players do not apply to circuit teams. They are recruited directly by the team's coaching staff. Programs identify highly ranked players in their region and invite them. Most invitations come from high school or independent-circuit performance — not from open tryouts.7

    So the path onto a circuit team runs through playing well where he already is. Anyone offering guaranteed placement on a shoe-circuit roster in exchange for a fee is not describing how this works.

    DMV programs confirmed active in 2026

    These programs were verified as competing in major grassroots events during the 2026 spring and summer season.8,9 This is a starting point for research, not a ranking and not an endorsement — and circuit affiliations are renegotiated annually, so confirm current status directly.

    • Team Takeover — a DC-based grassroots program, confirmed as a Nike EYBL team, and fielding multiple squads including National and Showcase teams.8,10
    • Team Durant — competed in the 2026 East Spring Circuit and East Mania.9
    • Team Thrill — competed in the 2026 East Spring Circuit, East Mania and DMV Live.9
    • DC Blue Devils — founded in the 1980s, now serving over 120 youth athletes in the Washington DC area, and competing in the 2026 East Spring Circuit.9,11

    Regional events these programs played in — the East Spring Circuit (April–May), East Mania and DMV Live in Baltimore — indicate where DMV club basketball concentrates in the spring.9 Note that event listings change every year; treat these as the shape of a season, not as dates to plan around.

    Guidance — not a rule

    • Ask what's included before paying anything. Programs vary enormously in cost, travel expectations and what families are asked to cover. Get it in writing.
    • Ask which events the team is committed to for the coming season, and check those against the NCAA evaluation windows above. A team that plays outside evaluation periods offers development, not exposure.
    • Ask where last year's seniors went. A program that produces college placements will answer specifically and quickly.
    • Confirm BBCS handling. The team must add him to its bench and roster, but the account is yours to create.

    Honesty

    What this page does not yet contain

    No specific camps, showcases or DMV-area events are listed here, and that is deliberate for now.

    Event data is the most perishable content in this entire hub — dates move, events are cancelled, organisers change, and a family that drives to a cancelled showcase or misses a registration deadline has been actively harmed by a stale list. Publishing an unverified event calendar would be worse than publishing nothing.

    What's needed to close this properly:

    • The NCAA's own list of certified events, which is maintained in the Basketball Certification System and is the authoritative source for what counts.5
    • The 2026–27 evaluation windows, read from the official calendar PDF and turned into a dated list.2
    • DMV club programs beyond the four named above — and for all of them, current circuit affiliation, cost, travel expectations and contacts, none of which are verified here.
    • Local college camp dates for the dozen-plus Division I programs within driving distance — these publish annually on each athletics site.

    Guidance — not a rule

    Until that exists, the highest-value source is not a website. It's his high school coach and his club coach, who know which regional events college staff actually attend and which ones simply advertise well. That knowledge is local, current, and free.

    Two things to do this week

    Download the official 2026–27 recruiting calendar and put every evaluation window into a shared family calendar. That single document tells you which months matter and which camps are worth money.

    Start the BBCS registration. It's free, it takes longer than expected because of the document uploads, and without it he cannot step onto the floor at the events where coaches actually evaluate.

    And one thing not to do: don't go looking for a club team to pay. Circuit teams recruit players; they don't sell places. The work that gets him onto one is the work he's already doing at his own school.
